# Comparative Study of Casting and Laser Directed Energy Deposition (L-DED) for Aluminum Alloy 1575

## Abstract 

My project compared  microstructure and mechanical properties of marine-grade Al-Mg alloy 1575 produced using two methods: conventional casting followed by cold rolling and an additive manufacturing technique called laser directed energy deposition (L-DED). The cast samples were melted in an induction furnace, then  modified with an Al-Ti10 master alloy, then exposed to cold rolling to remove porosity. L-DED samples were built from gas-atomized aluminium 1575 powder under an argon  atmosphere. Dog-bone shaped  specimens from both groups were tested in uniaxial tension (using the machine Shimadzu AGS-X, 1 mm/min) and examined by optical metallography after standard grinding, polishing and etching using  Keller’s method.

  As a result of the experiments, I found that L-DED samples showed a fine, directionally aligned fibrous microstructure because of  rapid, highly directional solidification with cooling rates of 10³–10⁶ K/s, whereas the cast samples demonstrated  rough, equiaxial dendritic structure with scattered porosity. Their  lower dislocation density caused a lower yield strength, while their pores acted as stress concentrators, promoting early crack initiation and reducing ductility. This led to  a significant  mechanical advantage: L-DED samples reached an average yield strength of 402 MPa, which is 3.1× higher than casting results (128 MPa)  - and ultimate tensile strength value of 465 MPa, which is 2.2× higher than those of casting-produced samples that got only 215 MPa. By also showing greater ductility (elongation by 8.9% against 4.1% ) and twice the reduction of area (10% against 5%), L-DED method demonstrates its clear dominance. Important to note, the L-DED results of 402 MPa yield strength, 465 MPa ultimate tensile strength exceeded even the standard GOST specifications for wrought (rolled) 1575 alloy which are 200–280 MPa and 370–440 MPa, respectively.

In conclusion, my research results demonstrate  that  1575 alloy components produced using L-DED technology have  properties superior to those produced by  traditional casting or even conventional wrought processing, confirming its potential in spheres where both strength and material efficiency matter, especially in  shipbuilding.
